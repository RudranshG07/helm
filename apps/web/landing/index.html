Detect

A bridge that is out tells you before it comes down.

A mandate is the standing permission that lets a merchant take money from a customer's account. India gives a failing one four crossings: the original charge and three retries. Miss all four and the bank tears it down, and the customer has to build it again from nothing. Helm scores every mandate the moment a charge fails, and again nightly before one does, so the gap is visible while it can still be closed.

Decide

Helm cannot add crossings. It decides when to take them.

A bridge that is under water for a morning is not the same as one that has been demolished, and spending your last crossing on the demolished one is how merchants lose customers they could have kept. Helm reads the decline, works out when that account is actually funded, and refuses attempts that cannot succeed.

  • Classify first. Soft, hard, or unmapped. A revoked mandate never gets a retry, it gets a request to re-authorise.
  • Cross when the gate is clear. On the first of the month every merchant in the country debits at once. Retries land when that customer's account has historically been funded, and never inside the windows where the whole market is queueing.
  • Rebuild, when no crossing can work. If the bridge is gone the customer gets a link to authorise a new one, in their own language, and can name the date they will have the money instead of failing again.
Prove

Sixteen rules run after the model, and refusals are logged as loudly as approvals.

The model proposes an action and a reason. It never moves money. A deterministic policy engine checks every proposal against the attempt budget, the notice period, the execution windows, consent, and the kill switch, and the first refusal wins. Every decision is recorded with the rule that produced it, so any single charge can be defended after the fact.
