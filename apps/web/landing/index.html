Detect

A mandate tells you it is dying before it dies.

India gives a failed mandate one original attempt and three retries. After that the bank cancels it and the customer has to authorise all over again. Helm scores every mandate the moment a charge fails, and again nightly before one does, so the window is visible while it is still open.

Decide

Helm cannot add attempts. It decides how to spend them.

The default schedule spends the same three retries on a customer who was short for one day and on one who revoked the mandate entirely. Helm reads the decline code, works out when that account is likely funded, and refuses to spend an attempt that cannot succeed.

  • Classify first. Soft, hard, or unmapped. A revoked mandate never gets a retry, it gets a request to re-authorise.
  • Time it against evidence. Retries land when that customer's account has historically been funded, not on a fixed clock, and never inside a contested bank window.
  • Ask, when no retry can work. The customer gets a link to re-authorise, in their own language, and can name the date they will have the money instead of failing again.
Prove

Sixteen rules run after the model, and refusals are logged as loudly as approvals.

The model proposes an action and a reason. It never moves money. A deterministic policy engine checks every proposal against the attempt budget, the notice period, the execution windows, consent, and the kill switch, and the first refusal wins. Every decision is recorded with the rule that produced it, so any single charge can be defended after the fact.
